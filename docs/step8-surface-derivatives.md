# Moving surface points and tangent geometry

`raw.dnearp(state, a, b, c)` returns the nearest point on a triaxial ellipsoid and signed altitude. The six input components are Cartesian position followed by velocity. When the derivative exists, `found` is true and the result also has the three-component surface velocity and altitude rate. At a focal-set singularity the position and altitude are still available. For a sphere of radius 2, a point at (3, 4, 0) moving radially with velocity (3, 4, 0) has surface point (1.2, 1.6, 0), altitude 3, zero surface velocity and altitude rate 5. [NAIF DNEARP](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/toolkit_docs/C/cspice/dnearp_c.html)

`raw.surfpv(vertex, direction, a, b, c)` accepts two six-component states. The second contains the ray direction and that direction’s derivative. A forward intercept with a defined velocity yields the owned six-component intercept state. A missing intercept or a singular tangent velocity yields `{ found: false }`. Shape errors and an invalid vertex on the surface produce diagnostics. Positions and semiaxes use consistent length units. Derivatives use those units per input time unit. [NAIF SURFPV](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/toolkit_docs/C/cspice/surfpv_c.html)

`raw.tangpt(method, target, et, fixref, abcorr, corloc, observer, dref, dvec)` finds the point of closest approach on the forward viewing ray. Supply the ellipsoid method, a target-centered body-fixed frame, an observer, the direction’s reference frame, and the surface or tangent correction locus. The result contains the tangent point, altitude, observer range, surface point, target epoch and observer-to-surface vector. Positions and distances are in km. Epochs are TDB seconds past J2000. A forward intersection has zero altitude. A closest approach behind the observer gives a range of zero. Target radii, ephemerides and any non-inertial frame definitions must be available. [NAIF TANGPT](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/toolkit_docs/C/cspice/tangpt_c.html)
